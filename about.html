About

How Kitsu thinks, and what she’s made of.

A calculator is a small idea: one expression in, one number out. The interesting part is never the number. It is being able to see why that number came out, and to have somebody notice when you are about to make a mistake. That is the whole job here.

Using the calculator

Type in the box, or press keys. The number under the box is a live preview — it updates as you type, before you press Enter. Press Enter (or the = key) and Kitsu writes down every rung she climbed in the ∑ Steps panel.

From the keyboard

Everything on the keypad works from the keyboard, from anywhere on the page. No shortcuts to memorise, no modes.

Constants, and a few things worth knowing

Constants and behaviours
pi eType the name, or press the key. You may write the glyphs π and √ too, and the calculator reads them.
ansThe last answer, still there on the next line. Solve 512, then type ans + 1 and get 513.
implicit timesTwo things side by side multiply: 2π, 3(4+1), 2sin(30). The ANS key inserts ans for you.
glyphsYou can type + − × ÷ ^ ( ) ! and the calculator normalises them, so a symbol copied from a maths book works as-is.
degreesA ° after a number is ignored, so sin(30°) and sin(30) agree.

The fn row

The orange fn key swaps the keypad between two pages of keys. The main page carries what you need day to day — sin cos tan π, ln log √, the digits, + − × ÷, the brackets, ! and ANS. The second page holds the rest:

What the fn row holds
sin⁻¹ cos⁻¹ tan⁻¹write their bracketed names, so asin(0.5) needs no keyboard.
abs e mod gcd lcminsert the name with the bracket already open.
det nCr nPr hypotthe same, for the arithmetic you would otherwise have to write out.

± x² n! 1/xact on whatever is already in the box — flip its sign, square it, count it, or turn it into its reciprocal.
xᵇthe same ^ as the main page.

The ladder

Order of operations is not a pile, it is a ladder, and Kitsu climbs it from the bottom rung up. Tightest binds first:

Operators from tightest binding to loosest
RungWritesReads
factorial5!120
power2 ^ 3 ^ 22 ^ (3 ^ 2) = 512
unary minus−2 ^ 2−4, because the sign sits below the power
implicit times2pi   3(4+1)   2sin(30)side-by-side means multiply
times and divide12 ÷ 3 × 28, chained from the left
plus and minus2 + 3 − 41, the loosest rung

Because implicit multiplication binds tighter than division, 1/2pi means 1/(2π). If you want the other reading, write it: (1/2) * pi.

Kitsu’s mood sheet

Four moods, and she is never hiding which one she is in.

  • idle

    Waiting. She blinks somewhere between every 1.6 and 5 seconds, and her eyes follow your pointer.

    “Blink twice if you need me.”

  • think

    You are mid-expression — there is a bracket open, or a trailing operator. She is reading ahead.

    “Order of operations is a ladder, not a pile. Climbing…”

  • happy

    It landed, and she wants you to check the steps. Also what happens when you click her, for 1.7 seconds.

    “Solved — and I showed my work.”

  • oops

    Something could not be evaluated. She tilts five degrees and never, ever says you did it wrong.

    “A bracket went missing. I counted twice.”

Every line she can say when it goes wrong

There are exactly seven failure modes and exactly one line for each. None of them blame you.

Kitsu’s error lines
unclosedA bracket went missing. I counted twice.
div0Division by zero. Even I can’t outrun that one.
negsqrtSquare root of a negative? Not on the real number line, friend.
tan90tan(90 degrees) runs off to infinity — undefined.
domainThat leaves the real number line, so I stopped there.
factI can only count whole numbers, and only up to 170 of them.
unknownI don’t know that one. Try sin, cos, tan, ln, log, sqrt, abs, pi, e or ans.

There is no path that can produce NaN on this site. If a value comes out non-finite it is a domain error, never a number you have to be told to distrust.

The background

Every page shares one fixed canvas behind the content, and it is doing something. It paints graph paper at 48 px, with every fifth line a shade darker, and the horizontal lines move with the page so it feels like paper you are working on.

Down the whole length of the page runs an ink curve. You ink it by scrolling: the further you go, the further the ink goes, and Kitsu’s orange nib is at the tip of it with a trail that stretches when you scroll faster. A dashed pencil sketch runs slightly ahead of the ink, and six enormous maths glyphs drift past in the margins at their own pace.

If your system asks for reduced motion, all of that goes static: the canvas paints once, fully inked, and nothing on any page animates afterwards.

Changelog

  1. 1.1 · 2 October 2026

    • The keypad rebuilt to five columns and six rows, so every day-to-day function — sin cos tan π ln log √ ^ — sits on the main page and the second page holds the rest.
    • New functions: det for 2×2 and 3×3 determinants, plus mod, gcd, lcm, nCr, nPr, hypot, round, floor, ceil and sign. Functions may now take a comma-separated list of arguments.
    • Arguments count is checked, so a wrong number of them is a plain domain error instead of a wrong answer.
    • A step now shows the value an operand climbed to, so 2 + 3 × 4 reads 2 + 12 = 14, and a factorial spells out its product.
    • The page wipe can always be escaped, and the steps sheet can be reached and dismissed on a phone.

  2. 1.0 · 1 October 2026

    • The calculator: live preview, history tape, steps drawer, DEG/RAD switch, five-column scientific keypad and the full keyboard map.
    • The engine: tokenizer, recursive-descent parser and a narrating evaluator, with implicit multiplication, right-associative powers, factorials and seven typed error codes.
    • Kitsu: four moods, blinking, pointer tracking, a hop when you click her, and an opt-in voice with word-by-word highlighting in her bubble.
    • Speech input, where the browser supports it: “two plus three times four” becomes 2 + 3 * 4.
    • The solving-paper background on all three pages, and a full static mode for prefers-reduced-motion.

Deliberately not here

No accounts, no sync, no history that outlives your tab, no analytics and no cookies. The tape is memory for the length of one visit, and it goes when you close it.
